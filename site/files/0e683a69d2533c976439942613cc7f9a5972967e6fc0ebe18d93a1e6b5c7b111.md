# Offset-clamshell pusher: 50 m/s impact report

**Candidate:** supplied 350 mm Lower + Upper clamshell assembly. **Analysis:** ANSYS LS-DYNA explicit transient, two printed flexible shells, four ideal local closure joints, two motor masses, original two-spreader / eight-inch-foam fixture. **Observation:** 8.000 ms. **Status:** the completed 4 mm case passes the single-case numerical screens. The 4 → 3 mm candidate mesh screen **fails**. Failed measures: Bolt-collar mean relative motion.

The modeled pusher first stops at **5.273 ms after 122.65 mm**, with a **251.55 kN** short initial pusher-to-spreader force peak. Sampled central seam opening reaches **9.88 mm**; joint hardware capacity remains unqualified.

This is a comparative elastic demand study. It does not predict printed-part survival, bolt strength, preload, slip, physical rebound or commercial foam capacity.

## 1. Supplied geometry and assembly

The supplied assembly STEP has two valid solids. It was meshed as **two independent flexible parts** with their offset seam and inter-shell contact. The one-piece historical master was not used. Read-only native SolidWorks volumes and source hashes are in the [CAD inspection](data/cad-inspection.json) and [native verification](data/native-verification.json). The native assembly and supplied STEP volumes differ by about 0.000053%; the native shell save times postdate the STEP exports, so feature-level revision identity cannot be proved from volume alone.

## 2. Physical model and dynamic load path

The pusher enters at **50 m/s in −Z**. Printed material uses density 1060 kg/m³, isotropic elastic E = 1806.6 MPa and assumed Poisson ratio 0.35. Printed shells weigh **579.77 g in the 4 mm FE mesh**; with motors, moving mass is **741.37 g** and nominal initial kinetic energy is **926.72 J**. The mesh’s printed volume exceeds the STEP volume by about 0.8%; the exact per-shell errors remain in the [setup audit](data/setup-audit.json).

Each initially stationary aluminum spreader is 41.275 × 152.4 × 8 mm and 135.87 g. Two 203.2 mm unconfined foam blocks use the reference MAT057 nonlinear synthetic compression/unloading curve (54.46 kg/m³, 0.30337 MPa at 25% compression). Plate-to-foam friction is zero; pusher-to-plate and inter-shell friction are 0.1. The initial pad gap is 0.1 mm. No axial travel limit, ongoing motor thrust, motor casing stiffness, failure law or bolt preload is included. See the [complete model and material curve](data/candidate-model.json) and [solved keyword deck](data/candidate-input.k).

**Load path:** moving shells and motors → two finite-mass spreaders → nonlinear foam → fixed rear backing. Force peaks at these locations occur at different times because the spreaders accelerate and the foam deforms.

## 7. Numerical evidence and qualification

The solver reached 8.000 ms with normal termination; the [execution record](data/candidate-execution.json) and [termination evidence](data/solver-termination.json) are distinct from the single-case numerical assessment. Energy-balance error is 0.0133% of initial energy. Contact impulse and moving-momentum discrepancy is 0.0037%. Maximum summed absolute nonfriction contact energy is 1.385%. Added mass is 1.961 mg. The minimum saved-state foam Gauss-point relative Jacobian is 0.371; no sampled inversion or element deletion was detected.

The repaired 4 mm mesh contains 78,906 pusher tetrahedra. Its minimum tetrahedral altitude is 0.110 mm after 541 local contractions; no altitude is below 0.05 mm. Separate shells have no shared nodes, and both impact patches and four closure groups passed the [pre-solve setup audit](data/setup-audit.json).

The saved-state planar pusher/plate penetration screen reaches 0.394 mm left and 0.403 mm right. The seam contact generated initial incident-contact detections; the largest reported overlap is 0.00118 mm. These sampled/initial values do not bound unsaved-cycle or curved-surface penetration.

## 9. Engineering interpretation and limits

The model supports a same-fixture comparison of stopping travel, load path and elastic demand **within its numerical checks**. The candidate has a physically meaningful offset seam in the model, and the sampled seam motion deserves joint-specific hardware review. A 50 m/s impact can generate a much larger short-duration pusher-to-spreader peak than the foam/backing peak because the spreaders begin at rest. Foam capacity and actual printed-part safety remain unverified.

The assumed PA12-CF10 isotropic elastic law omits print anisotropy, moisture, rate dependence and damage. The synthetic MAT057 foam curve is anchored at one catalog point, without high-rate product calibration. Ideal cable guides omit clearance, friction and cable compliance. Bolts lack geometry, preload and strength; seam contact stiffness and friction are assumed. Support-frame compliance, continuing motor thrust, fracture/deletion and repeated impact are absent. Mesh and timestep checks are limited as stated above. The fixed stress color range intentionally saturates above 160 MPa and must not be read as a failure threshold.
