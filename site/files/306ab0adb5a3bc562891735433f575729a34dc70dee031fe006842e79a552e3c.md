# Six-inch depth case: timestep-collapse diagnosis

The run failed to complete because its timestep collapsed at approximately 3.6265 ms. This was a numerical progress stall, not a licensing failure. The prescribed 8 ms event was not completed; this case cannot establish six-inch stopping performance or a successful depth comparison.

The preserved `messag` records a timestep of 73.3 ns at 3.6200 ms, then 5.24e-17 s at cycle 45000 with time still 3.6265 ms. Subsequent cycles reached approximately 485000 while physical time remained unchanged to the reported precision and the timestep fell to 2.54e-18 s. Roughly 109 minutes elapsed between the first clearly stalled log entry and the final entry. The outer process timeout finally stopped the owned solver.

## Location and evidence

The strongest evidence points to a local instability/deformation collapse in the **left flexible cable-cap sidewall/contact wedge, part 2**, initially near X = -115.5 mm, Y = 20–21 mm, Z = 33–39 mm. The exact governing element at the unsaved collapse cycle is **not established**: GLSTAT labels the controller `dt2ms`, and the saved binary states end at 3.619982334 ms, about 6.5 microseconds before the stall.

- Maximum saved nodal speed increased from 221.9 m/s at 3.6000 ms, through 153.5 m/s at 3.60994 ms, to 548.3 m/s at 3.619982 ms. The extreme motion is in the cap sidewall/contact wedge.
- Cap tetrahedron **59581**, initially centered at (-115.512, 20.104, 35.553) mm, has a linearized time to zero volume of **5.37 microseconds**, calculated as V / (-dV/dt) from its last saved coordinates and velocities. Neighbor **63682** gives 9.78 microseconds; **59569** gives 10.97 microseconds. These are localization diagnostics, not assertions of subsequent element inversion.
- All body and cap tetrahedra remain positive at the last saved state. Minimum current/initial volume ratios are 0.9382 for the body and 0.7757 for the cap. Cap element 63558 has a small current altitude of 0.1134 mm and strong localized deformation; the independent material review identifies it as the minimum-stretch location.
- All eight Gauss points of every foam element remain positive at the last saved state; minimum relative Jacobian is 0.38715. The evidence does **not** identify global foam bottoming as the cause.
- GLSTAT at 3.62600 ms still reports total energy 813.750 J versus approximately 812.965 J initial effective kinetic energy, but local collapse can occur before global energy becomes a useful warning. Added mass remains 5.713 mg; startup mass scaling did not keep adding mass during the stall.

The cap/contact region is implicated. The independent geometry review locates this at the left outer cap sidewall: cap plane X = -116 mm faces body plane X = -116.2 mm across a nominal 0.2 mm mating gap, adjacent to an angled internal surface. This is not a bolt collar; the bolt stations at Z = 7.94 and 68.26 mm are remote from the hotspot. Existing evidence does not distinguish an under-resolved contact transient from a local large-deformation instability of the elastic cap idealization. It does not establish physical fracture, a real material failure threshold, or a valid stopping result.

## Bounded next diagnostic

Preserve this failed run. The preferred minimal route to a usable depth sensitivity is a guarded six-inch case on the already verified **3 mm mesh**, compared directly with the completed **3 mm eight-inch case, mesh-guided-v2**. Keep the physical material, contact, and guide definitions identical. This refines the localized cap/contact discretization while preserving an identical mesh within the resulting depth comparison. Completion and passing checks would support a depth sensitivity on that mesh; they would not prove that the 4 mm failure was caused solely by mesh size, or establish six-inch mesh convergence.

An alternative diagnostic keeps the original 4 mm six-inch mesh with a **45 ns nominal timestep**. Since 45 ns remains above the original 28.04 ns contact warning, a surviving result still needs warning review; **22.5 ns** is the appropriate smaller-step follow-up if the guarded case still develops the local instability or late-contact sensitivity remains unresolved. Neither mesh refinement nor a smaller timestep is a guaranteed repair. Do not change ongoing mass scaling, erosion, or material failure rules to force completion.

Add a whole-job minimum-timestep termination guard to new cases:

```text
*CONTROL_TERMINATION
0.008,0,0.01
```

R16 Keyword Manual, volume I, *CONTROL_TERMINATION, pp. 12-553–12-554, defines field 3 `DTMIN` as a scale factor: minimum timestep = solver-determined initial timestep × DTMIN. Reaching this minimum terminates the job with a restart dump. With a 45 ns initial timestep, 0.01 corresponds to approximately 0.45 ns; verify the actual initial solver step in each run. The existing failed input leaves DTMIN at its zero default.

**Necessary exception check:** `ERODE=1` on *CONTROL_TIMESTEP, or nonzero `PSFAIL` on *CONTROL_SOLID, changes this behavior to solid erosion. The preserved case has ERODE = 0 and no nonzero PSFAIL. Keep both conditions for a whole-job guard; do not use erosion or ongoing mass scaling to conceal the collapse. Guard termination must remain an incomplete/failed assessment, even if the solver reports an orderly termination and writes a restart.

For automatic monitoring, an independent progress guard can request orderly SW1 termination when the owned run advances less than 0.1 microseconds over 30 seconds after previously making normal progress. Treat this as a diagnostic abort and preserve logs. This secondary criterion is a suggested monitor, not an implemented change here. The keyword guard should normally catch the collapse earlier and provide more useful restart evidence.
