Results

Local cable-closure pusher impact: bounded geometry iteration

Geometry: original continuous body with two local T closures; revised 5.5 mm closure stem. Solver: ANSYS LS-DYNA explicit, 50 m/s, 8 ms. Numerical screening: all listed single-case checks passed; mesh and timestep convergence remain unresolved. Engineering status: provisional elastic demand, not physical survival qualification.

The supplied parent geometry stopped at 0.334 ms when an element in the right closure's thin front cable wall drove the timestep to 1.76×10⁻¹⁵ s. The revised geometry completed the requested event. The wider stem therefore improved numerical robustness, but the matched early stress screen shows higher closure P99 demand. A second, narrower plug trial also stopped early at 0.178 ms. Neither geometry revision is accepted as a stress improvement; the supplied parent CAD remains the next optimization seed. The original parent has no valid full-event result to compare against this full run.

Geometry and load path

The revised assembly keeps one printed main body, separate left/right closures and eight generic bolt/nut envelopes. The two motor bores remain at X=±133.25 mm; cable guides remain at X=±101.6 mm with 9.8298/6.75 mm stepped openings. The closure stem half-width rises from 4.3 to 5.5 mm, with a matching body slot half-width of 5.7 mm. T shoulders, bolt axes and exterior fit remain fixed. The printed CAD mass is 561.182 g, down 0.103 g from the supplied parent; hardware envelope mass is 34.002 g. All 19 STEP solids reimport and all three parts pass actual Bambu X1C slicing and generated extrusion-path checks. These checks do not establish printed strength or real hardware capacity.

The delivery contains the wider-stem STEP assembly, the narrow-plug trial assembly and the retained parent STEP assembly. Their separate component STEP files and build parameters are packaged alongside them. The parent is selected for the next iteration because neither revision demonstrated lower closure demand through a completed comparable event.

The model uses three independent flexible solids, frictional body/closure contact, and eight ideal clamped bolt collars carrying the CAD-derived generic hardware mass. Two 80 g motor masses are distributed over the bore nodes. The printed material is the prior study's isotropic elastic proxy (1060 kg/m³, E=1806.6 MPa, ν=0.35). The carrier and motor housings are excluded. The cable guides use eight transverse weighted-average equations and leave axial motion free; real cable compliance, diameter, tension and slip are not modeled.

The provisional fixture deliberately repeats the latest completed clamshell simulation: two initially stationary 41.275 × 152.4 × 8 mm aluminum spreaders at X=±152.3825 mm, 203.2 mm unconfined foam, 0.1 mm initial contact gap, friction 0.1 at pusher/spreader and closure interfaces, and fixed axial rear foam faces. The synthetic MAT057 foam has 54.46 kg/m³ density and 0.30337 MPa at 25% compression; it is not the proposed 30 kg/m³ foam product or a calibrated crush curve. The 346.04 mm spreader outside span also differs from the new nominal 350 mm stop span. This fixture is for direct comparison with the prior numerical model.

Comparison and qualification

The parent and revision share only a valid 0–0.299 ms window. In that window, closure-left P99 rises +54.4% and closure-right P99 rises +27.9%; impact-pod-root P99 changes +0.3%. These are directional coarse-mesh observations, not a qualified stress ranking. The parent then collapses numerically. See the early-window comparison.

The independent narrow-plug trial reduced the closure stem half-width to 3.2 mm and body slot half-width to 3.6 mm while preserving the cable installation sweep. It passed STEP and X1C toolpath checks, but a right closure element near the rear guide end (initial Z≈73.5 mm) drove the timestep to 2.66×10⁻¹⁵ s at 0.178 ms. Its execution record, partial metrics and element diagnosis are diagnostic evidence only. It never reached the stopping event and cannot be ranked against the completed run.

Next geometry decision

  1. Redesign the removable guide as a thicker load-bearing insert or split sleeve around the cable, with a continuous front/rear lip and explicit bearing path into the main body. Retain the cable opening and installation path. The narrow printed end lips should not carry the idealized guide reaction through one thin edge.
  2. Rework fastener load transfer with selected bolts, washers or inserts, adequate edge distances, and modeled shank bearing/preload. Widening the stem alone consumes material beside the current hole pattern; keep the present wider-stem variant as an unsuccessful experiment.
  3. Round/gusset the motor pod-to-bridge transition and verify spreader contact reach under the actual 350 mm fixture. This is the next credible region for stress reduction after the guide is stabilized.
  4. Consider mass removal from the central bridge only after left/right-leading and roll cases. The aligned run shows lower central demand but does not establish torsional reserve.

The next agent should use CONTINUE.md, select real foam and aluminum hardware when available, model compliant cable contact and hardware bearing, and rerun matched parent/revision cases before accepting a design. Per the user's updated standard, that next printed-part analysis mesh is to be generated in Altair HyperMesh with conforming hexahedral volume elements and quadrilateral surface faces. The current tetrahedral result remains historical evidence and cannot be relabeled as a hex solve.
